# Analytics Personas

## Persona Classes

Analytics users fall into three broad classes:

- Investigators: actively drill into data to explain behavior.
- Reviewers: periodically check performance, exceptions, and trends.
- Executives: consume concise summaries and decision signals.

## Energy Manager

Class: Investigator.

Goals:

- Understand consumption and demand trends.
- Identify abnormal usage.
- Compare sites, buildings, and meters.
- Reduce avoidable energy cost.

Recurring questions:

- Why did consumption increase yesterday?
- Which building drove the peak?
- Is the current load profile normal?
- Which meters are consistently inefficient?

Decisions:

- operational adjustments,
- equipment scheduling,
- tariff review,
- maintenance escalation,
- conservation actions.

Success criteria:

- can explain changes with evidence,
- can isolate drivers quickly,
- can export or share findings.

Cadence:

- daily operational review,
- weekly trend review,
- monthly management reporting.

## Plant Manager

Class: Reviewer.

Goals:

- Understand production-adjacent energy patterns.
- Identify operational periods that create demand spikes.
- Confirm whether site behavior is stable.

Recurring questions:

- Did energy use match expected operations?
- Which shifts or operating windows increased demand?
- Are outages or stale telemetry hiding real behavior?

Decisions:

- schedule changes,
- equipment operating policy,
- escalation to engineering or maintenance.

Success criteria:

- sees clear daily/weekly comparisons,
- understands operational impact without deep report knowledge.

Cadence:

- daily or weekly.

## Building Administrator

Class: Reviewer.

Goals:

- Monitor building-level energy behavior.
- Compare tenants, areas, or meter groups.
- Detect unusual usage after hours.

Recurring questions:

- Which building used more than expected?
- Is usage high outside operating hours?
- Are meters assigned to the correct context?

Decisions:

- building operations changes,
- tenant or department follow-up,
- maintenance ticket creation.

Success criteria:

- can interpret building comparisons without raw-data expertise.

Cadence:

- weekly and monthly.

## Engineering

Class: Investigator.

Goals:

- Analyze load profiles, demand curves, power factor, and equipment behavior.
- Investigate anomalies and root causes.
- Validate changes after interventions.

Recurring questions:

- When did peak demand occur?
- Was the peak isolated or sustained?
- Did power factor degrade during a specific period?
- Does the load profile suggest equipment cycling or abnormal operation?

Decisions:

- equipment tuning,
- power quality investigation,
- maintenance intervention,
- metering validation.

Success criteria:

- high-resolution data is inspectable,
- assumptions and aggregation rules are visible,
- missing or incomplete data is clearly identified.

Cadence:

- incident-driven and project-based.

## Sustainability Officer

Class: Reviewer.

Goals:

- Track consumption reduction and efficiency initiatives.
- Compare baselines against actuals.
- Support sustainability reporting.

Recurring questions:

- Are we improving against baseline?
- Which buildings improved or regressed?
- Are changes weather, occupancy, or operationally driven?

Decisions:

- sustainability initiative prioritization,
- reporting statements,
- investment recommendations.

Success criteria:

- comparisons are trustworthy,
- baseline logic is explicit,
- incomplete data is not mistaken for improvement.

Cadence:

- monthly and quarterly.

## Finance

Class: Reviewer.

Goals:

- Understand cost drivers.
- Review demand peaks and tariff impact.
- Support budgeting and allocation.

Recurring questions:

- Which site or building drives cost?
- Did demand charges explain the bill increase?
- Are anomalies material enough to investigate?

Decisions:

- budget allocation,
- cost recovery,
- tariff review,
- financial explanation.

Success criteria:

- energy behavior can be translated into cost context,
- outputs can be exported or shared.

Cadence:

- monthly billing and budget cycles.

## Auditor

Class: Investigator.

Goals:

- Verify data lineage and completeness.
- Confirm reported values can be traced.
- Identify missing or suspect intervals.

Recurring questions:

- Which readings support this calculation?
- Were there missing intervals?
- Was a value measured, calculated, estimated, or incomplete?

Decisions:

- accept or challenge a report,
- request remediation,
- document evidence.

Success criteria:

- provenance is visible,
- confidence levels are explicit,
- exports are traceable.

Cadence:

- audit cycles and exception reviews.

## Executive

Class: Executive.

Goals:

- Understand overall energy posture.
- See trend direction, risk, and opportunity.
- Avoid operational detail unless necessary.

Recurring questions:

- Are we improving?
- What changed materially?
- Where should attention or investment go?

Decisions:

- strategic prioritization,
- budget approval,
- accountability review.

Success criteria:

- concise, credible, evidence-backed summaries,
- clear trend and variance explanations.

Cadence:

- monthly, quarterly, and board-level review.
